The making of this site

The status page says how the archive works today. This page says how it got here: what was built when, who it is built for, and what their path through the site changed.

What was built, and when

  1. 11/07/2026The pilot. Tebhaga and Nachole as pins on a map with a timeline, filters for phones and a page for each entry. The content was typed by hand into JSON files.
  2. 11/07 to 14/08/2026A Sanity Studio was set up as the place to edit content, and the pilot's entries were imported into it.
  3. 04/11/2026The site began reading a snapshot pulled from Sanity. The design brief and the Bible, the numbered rules for every entry and screen, were written.
  4. 17/11/2026The three code repositories were merged into one. The status page was added. The site was rebuilt around the four readers described below: a searchable list first, the map on request, and an entry page that explains itself.

Who it is for

Four people, in the order the brief ranks them. They are invented. They are working assumptions drawn from the brief and the Bible, not findings from interviews, and they should be replaced by what real readers say once the site has been tested with a handful of people.

A fifth person uses the site too: the editor. Today that is the maintainer; later, verified editors. Their page is the status page.

Their journeys

Each journey follows one person through five stages. Under each stage: what the site did before 17/11/2026, what it does now, and what is still open.

What the journeys showed

  1. The entry page is the front door. Three of the four people land on an entry, not on the home page. So each entry now says what it is, when and where, and how well it is sourced, before anything else.
  2. The home page asked people to decode a map. Dots with no labels help someone who already knows the story. The home page is now a sentence, a search box and a list. The map is one tap away for those who want it.
  3. Most of the archive could not be reached. Only movements and events with a pin appeared on the home page, so people, organisations, places and texts were hidden. Every entry is now in the list and in search.
  4. Trust has to be visible where the claim is. A reader should not need the status page to learn that an entry rests on one source. Every entry carries that line itself, and unverified entries say so in the list.
  5. The site spoke its own internal language. Codes such as pe_ila_mitra, the word "entity" and labels such as named_in have been replaced by names and plain words.
  6. Doubt is not an error. Open questions and disagreements were shown in red, like faults. They are now dashed and hatched boxes. Red is kept for the movement itself (Bible V1).

What design alone cannot fix

The journeys also end in places a redesign cannot reach. These are the open items, with the work each one needs.

Needs writing and editing

  • Bangla. No entry has Bangla text, and the first reader reads Bangla first.
  • Sources. 35 of 82 entries have no source the archive accepts.
  • "Since then". Nothing connects a movement to the present (Bible H2).
  • Ordinary participants and communities. Mostly leaders are named (Bible P1, P2).

Needs building

  • Link previews and search engines. Entries need to be generated as real pages (plan D9).
  • A correction form that needs no account (Bible C2, P5).
  • Page numbers and disputed claims shown side by side (plan D7, Bible E2).
  • The map: no modern borders before 1947, shapes as well as colours, a region filter that reads then-and-now districts (Bible G1, G2, V2).

The order of this work is in docs/PLAN.md.

What it is made with

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework. Leaflet draws the map on OpenStreetMap tiles. Content is edited in Sanity and copied into the site as JSON files. The fonts are Fraunces and IBM Plex. Every tool in the chain is free to use. How the pieces connect is on the status page.
